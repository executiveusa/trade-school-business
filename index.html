The entry offer

Start with a feasibility and funding-readiness assessment.

The first decision is not which grant to apply for. It is whether the training model makes business sense, which approvals apply, and who would pay for the training.

COMMERCIAL CASE

Demand and economics

Define the employer or student buyer, proposed trade, capacity, training costs and revenue assumptions.

APPROVAL MAP

Licensing and delivery

Identify questions for Nevada regulators, delivery requirements and whether an existing provider partnership is more practical.

FUNDING READINESS

Program eligibility research

Screen relevant workforce pathways, responsible agencies, documentation needs and the difference between eligibility and actual funding.

DECISION

Prioritized action plan

Compare routes, unresolved risks, verification steps and milestones before committing to facilities, staffing or enrollment.

Three possible routes

You may not need to open a school first.

Partner with a provider

Explore a relationship with an existing appropriately approved training organization. Confirm program fit, responsibilities and economics.

Train through an employer

Evaluate structured workplace training, apprenticeship or on-the-job training pathways. Any reimbursement depends on program rules and prior approvals.

Launch a private school

Assess licensing, facilities, instruction, enrollment, financial requirements and any separate workforce-provider eligibility process.

How we evaluate an opportunity

Evidence before investment.

Define the buyer

Who needs the skills, who pays, and what would make this training valuable?

Verify the rules

Check the applicable Nevada regulator and workforce agency for the specific model.

Model the business

Stress-test enrollment or employer demand, delivery costs, timing and downside cases.

Choose the route

Proceed, pilot with a partner, revise the model or decide not to launch.
